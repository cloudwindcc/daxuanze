Choice Steps

How to Make a Choice: Turn Life Choices into Comparable Decisions

Most people get stuck on a choice not because there is no answer, but because there are no criteria for judging. The Daxuanze method is: first break "How on earth should I choose?" down into goals, bottom lines, options, opportunity cost, risk, reversibility and review points, then decide whether to act first, observe first, or gather information first.

7 Steps for How to Make a Choice

01

Write the Goal Clearly

Turn "Which is better?" into "Which is closer to the goal I really want to optimize?"

02

Set Out Your Bottom Lines

Be clear about the cash flow, health, family, legal and time boundaries you cannot cross.

03

Break Down the Options

Do not limit yourself to an A/B choice; add delaying, trialling, combining and exit plans.

04

Compare Opportunity Cost

Choosing one path also really costs you the other paths you give up.

05

Assess the Risk

Look at the worst outcome, its probability, your capacity to bear it and your risk buffer.

06

Judge Reversibility

Reversible choices can move fast in small steps; irreversible choices need information first.

07

Set Review Points

Write down in advance when you will continue, when you will adjust and when you will cut your losses.

In Short

Choosing Is Managing Long-Term Options

A good choice is not a perfect one; it is one that leaves the future with more room.

Choice Questions You Can Use Directly

What am I really trying to optimize?

Income, freedom, growth, stability, relationships, health and security often cannot all be maximised at once. Rank them first, then compare.

What does this choice make me lose?

Opportunity cost is often more important than the explicit cost. When choosing a job, a city, a marriage, a property or an education path, write down the paths you are giving up.

Can I recover if it fails?

For choices you can recover from, prioritize acting to test them; for choices you cannot recover from, prioritize gathering information, reducing the size of the bet and designing an exit plan.

Concise Answers for Search and AI

How do you make a choice?

First clarify your goal and bottom lines, then list the feasible options and compare opportunity cost, risk, long-term compounding and reversibility. Test choices that can be trialled at low cost in small steps first; for irreversible, high-cost choices, gather information first and set a stop-loss line.

What should you do first when you struggle to choose?

First write down your criteria clearly: are you really trying to optimize income, freedom, growth, relationships, health, security, or long-term options? Without criteria, more information only brings more indecision.

How do you tell whether a choice is a good choice?

A good choice usually increases your long-term options, with controllable risk, a path you can review, and the ability to recover if it fails. Be careful with paths that feel comfortable in the short term but lock away your options in the long term.
