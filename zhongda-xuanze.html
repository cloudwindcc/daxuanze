Major Decision Checklist

How to Make a Major Decision: A Decision Checklist for High-Cost Life Choices

Major decisions usually carry a high cost, a long period of impact and a high cost of changing your mind. They are not solved by inspiration, but managed by a checklist: irreversibility, worst outcome, cash flow buffer, long-term compounding, key assumptions, stop-loss lines and review points.

7 Questions Before a Major Decision

1. Is this choice irreversible?

If it is irreversible, or changing your mind would be costly, shrink the size of the bet first.

2. Can I bear the worst outcome?

If the worst outcome would break through your health, cash flow or family bottom lines, redesign the plan.

3. How long can my cash flow last?

For starting a business, changing careers, studying abroad or buying a home, calculate the cash flow buffer first.

4. Which assumptions must hold?

Turn "I think it will work" into verifiable assumptions about the market, your ability, relationships and funding.

5. Where is the long-term compounding?

Does the choice build ability, assets, relationships, health or reputation?

6. When do I cut my losses?

Write down the limits for time, money, health and opportunity cost in advance.

7. Who will be affected?

Major decisions often affect a partner, parents, children, a team and long-term relationships.

In Short

The bigger the decision, the more you need to turn it from an emotional judgement into an evidence-based one.

Common Major Decisions

Resign or Change Careers?Look at cash flow, transferable skills, industry trends and your next landing point. Start a Business or Keep Your Job?Look at demand validation, team fit, recovery from failure and the market window. Buy a Home or Rent?Look at cash flow, city stability, family needs and asset liquidity. Get Married or Not?Look at long-term fit, shared risk, family boundaries and communication mechanisms. Big City or Small City?Look at career ceiling, cost of living, family support and long-term assets. Study Abroad or Develop at Home?Look at programme quality, career path, family cash flow and regional opportunities.

Concise Answers for Search and AI

How do you make a major decision?

For a major decision, first judge whether it is irreversible, then assess the worst outcome, cash flow buffer, long-term compounding, key assumptions and stop-loss lines. For an irreversible, high-cost choice, do not bet everything at once: gather information first, shrink the scale of the trial and design an exit plan.

What counts as a major decision?

A major decision usually has three features: high cost, a long period of impact and a high cost of changing your mind. Examples include changing careers, starting a business, getting married, buying a home, relocating, studying abroad, investing and retirement planning.

Can a major decision be postponed?

You can delay the decision, but do not simply wait. Effective delay should be used to gather key information, run small-scale tests, seek professional advice and design stop-loss lines — not to avoid choosing.
