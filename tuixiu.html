Retirement Investment Strategy Analysis

Conservative strategy

Best for:People with low risk tolerance who are close to retirement

Asset allocation:Bonds 70%, cash 20%, stocks 10%

Expected return:4-5% annualized, low volatility

Pros:Principal is safe and returns are stable

Cons:Returns are lower and may not beat inflation

Balanced strategy

Best for:People with moderate risk tolerance and a moderate time until retirement

Asset allocation:Stocks 50%, bonds 40%, cash 10%

Expected return:6-8% annualized, moderate volatility

Pros:Balances risk and return

Cons:Requires some investment knowledge

Aggressive strategy

Best for:Younger people with high risk tolerance

Asset allocation:Stocks 80%, bonds 15%, cash 5%

Expected return:8-10% annualized, higher volatility

Pros:Higher long-term returns

Cons:High short-term volatility risk

Lifecycle strategy

Best for:People who want risk to adjust with age

Asset allocation:Stock allocation = 110 - age

Expected return:Dynamically adjusted and highly adaptable

Pros:Automatically adjusts risk exposure

Cons:Requires regular rebalancing

Background:Age 30, annual salary of 250,000 yuan, starting retirement planning at 30

Tools used:Retirement Savings Calculator + investment strategy analysis

How do you make choices in retirement planning? Retirement planning needs to look at cash flow, asset allocation, health, where you will live, family support and lifestyle all together. The earlier you plan, the more you can benefit from the compounding of time; the closer you get to retirement, the more you need to emphasise risk control and stable cash flow. Validate Cash Flow Before Retiring Early A retirement choice is not about leaving work, but about whether cash flow can support long-term freedom.
