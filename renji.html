A four-tier social circle strategy

Core circle (5 people)

Profile: Your closest family and most trusted friends

Investment focus: Deep emotional connection, unconditional support

Time allocation: 50% of social time

Maintenance cost: High, but with the greatest returns

Trust circle (15 people)

Profile: Good friends and important colleagues you contact often

Investment focus: Regular interaction, mutual benefit

Time allocation: 30% of social time

Maintenance cost: Medium, stable over the long term

Outer circle (50 people)

Profile: Ordinary friends and broad network contacts

Investment focus: Stay in touch, potential value

Time allocation: 15% of social time

Maintenance cost: Low, managed in batches

Acquaintance circle (150 people)

Profile: Casual acquaintances and industry contacts

Investment focus: Information exchange, value of weak ties

Time allocation: 5% of social time

Maintenance cost: Lowest, maintained via social media

When making a choice, should you listen to your parents or to yourself? Parents' views usually contain experience and warnings about risk, but the final choice has to come back to your own goals, constraints and capacity to bear the consequences. A better approach is neither simply to obey nor to rebel, but to turn the things your parents worry about into a checklist of risks you can verify. Should you end a draining relationship? To judge whether to end a draining relationship, see whether it has long drained your emotions, time and opportunity cost, and whether boundaries cannot be formed through communication. If the other person persistently denies the problem, crosses boundaries or keeps taking, reducing your investment protects your long-term state better than maintaining the relationship on the surface.
