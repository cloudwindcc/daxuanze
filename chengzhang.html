What should you look at when choosing a career? A career choice is not just about current pay. Also consider industry trends, how your abilities compound, your pace of growth, switching costs, cash-flow security and your future options. A path with high short-term income but no long-term growth in ability may not be the best choice. What should you assess before changing careers? Before changing careers, assess trends in the new industry, how transferable your abilities are, how far your income could fall, the learning period, the cost of experimenting and your exit plan. A safer approach is to validate on a small scale first — for example with a side project, a portfolio, industry interviews or a short internship — rather than cutting off your cash flow all at once. How do you decide whether to quit your job? To decide whether to quit, first see whether your reason for leaving is short-term emotion or a structural problem, then look at your cash-flow reserves, your next opportunity, growth in ability, the cost to your health and alternative options. If your next step is not yet clear, prioritize reducing risk while looking for a better path in parallel. How do you choose between two job offers? Do not compare two offers on salary alone. Compare industry trends, the quality of your manager, the role's responsibilities and authority, growth in ability, commuting cost, cash-flow security and your future options for changing jobs. Daxuanze recommends scoring short-term income and long-term compounding of ability separately, then seeing which option expands your future options more. How should you make career choices at 35? Career choices at 35 need to place more weight on cash-flow security, transferable abilities, industry cycles and family responsibilities. It is not that you cannot take risks, but that risk should become a controlled experiment: validate demand and income in the new direction first, then decide whether to switch full-time. Should you leave your current company? To judge whether to leave a company, see whether your current role is still increasing your ability, income, resources and future options. If it is only short-term discomfort, first try improving communication and responsibilities; if it is a long-term drain, growth has stalled and outside opportunities are better, prepare a departure plan with a buffer.
