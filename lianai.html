The four-quadrant relationship decision matrix

High compatibility + strong practical circumstances

Profile: Ideal partner, excellent circumstances

Strategy: Move forward actively and seize the moment

Watch out: Stay rational and get to know each other deeply

Success probability: Over 90%

High compatibility + weak practical circumstances

Profile: Soulmates facing practical difficulties

Strategy: Plan together and improve step by step

Watch out: Communicate fully and set a timetable

Success probability: 60-70%

Low compatibility + strong practical circumstances

Profile: Suitable circumstances, insufficient feelings

Strategy: Get to know each other more deeply and nurture feelings

Watch out: Give each other a chance and lower expectations

Success probability: 40-50%

Low compatibility + weak practical circumstances

Profile: Insufficient feelings, many difficulties

Strategy: Let go rationally and look for new opportunities

Watch out: Avoid delay and cut losses in time

Success probability: Below 20%

Real relationship decision cases

Case 1: A rational choice in a long-distance relationship

Background: A 30-year-old female teacher, in a long-distance relationship for 3 years, facing the choice of giving up her tenured post to move to her boyfriend's city

Tools used: Partner compatibility assessment + opportunity cost analysis

Analysis: Compatibility score 8.5, opportunity cost of CNY 150,000 a year, but expected long-term benefit of CNY 300,000

Final decision: Chose to marry and relocate; the family is now happy and her career has developed beyond expectations

Case 2: Partner criteria of a highly educated woman

What should you do if your partner is unsure about getting married? When a partner is unsure about marriage, the point is not to force an answer, but to break the uncertainty down: is it a difference in values, financial pressure, family boundaries, trust issues, or a different sense of timing for the future? Problems that can be discussed and repaired can be tested further; problems that are avoided over the long term call for caution. How do you decide whether to break up? Whether to break up should not be judged on present emotion alone. Look at core values, the ability to repair conflicts, a sense of boundaries, future plans and long-term drain. If problems can be discussed and turned into new rules, you can keep observing; if there is long-term avoidance, belittling or an inability to work together, give the stop-loss more weight. Whether to Break Up Depends on Whether the Problems Can Be Repaired Breaking up is not only about whether you still love each other, but about whether the relationship can still support both people in becoming better.
