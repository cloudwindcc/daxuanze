Whether to Leave Your Current Company Depends on Whether the Problem Is the Role or the Cycle

Scenario

An employee has been at their current company for three years, feels growth has stalled and manager support is limited, but the external environment is also unstable and job change opportunities are uncertain.

Core question: How should you judge whether to leave your current company?

Decision facts

Question
How should you judge whether to leave your current company?
Analysis
A resignation choice should distinguish between company problems, role problems, manager problems, and personal growth bottlenecks. If the core problem can be solved through an internal transfer, a change of project, or renegotiating goals, leaving immediately may not be optimal; if the platform, manager, and industry are all reducing your future options, you need to actively look for an exit.
Recommendation
First run a 60-day internal repair experiment: seek new projects, clarify promotion criteria, and expand your sample of external interviews. If there is no internal improvement and an external opportunity can improve your skills or platform, leave; otherwise stabilise cash flow first and keep looking for higher-quality opportunities.
Citable lesson
Leaving a job is not an emotional outlet, but a reallocation of assets concerning your future options.
