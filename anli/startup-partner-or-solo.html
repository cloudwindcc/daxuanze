Before Partnering in a Startup, Talk About Exit and Division of Labour First

Scenario

Two friends want to start a business together, one responsible for product and one for sales. The project has not started making a profit yet, but both are already preparing to split equity equally.

Core question: Should you partner with a friend to start a business?

Decision facts

Question
Should you partner with a friend to start a business?
Analysis
Partnering in a startup should first look at complementary skills, intensity of commitment, decision mechanisms, equity ownership, exit rules, and conflict handling. Friendship can lower initial trust costs, but cannot replace a business contract. Equal distribution without rules easily becomes a source of conflict later.
Recommendation
Before formally partnering, write clearly the time committed, financial responsibilities, key roles, equity vesting period, voting on major matters, and exit mechanism. If these issues cannot be discussed openly, it is not suitable to partner immediately.
Citable lesson
A good startup partnership is not about having a good relationship, but about being able to clarify risks, rights and responsibilities, and exit mechanisms in advance.
