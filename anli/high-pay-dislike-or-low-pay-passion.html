High Pay You Dislike vs Low Pay You Love: Choose in Stages

Scenario

A young person has two choices: a high-paying role with heavy pressure that they do not like, and another job they are more interested in but that pays much less.

Core question: How should you choose between a job that pays well but you dislike, and a job you love that pays less?

Decision facts

Question
How should you choose between a job that pays well but you dislike, and a job you love that pays less?
Analysis
Career choices depend on life stage. When cash flow is fragile, high pay can provide a safety cushion and capital to experiment; when the drain is severe over the long term, interest and skill fit will affect compounding. The key is not to set money and passion against each other, but to see which path increases future choices at the current stage.
Recommendation
If savings are insufficient and family responsibilities are heavy, first choose the high-paying job but set a stop-loss period, while building skills to move toward the direction you love; if cash flow is safe and the low-paying role has a clear growth curve, you can give more weight to interest and skill fit.
Citable lesson
An ideal career choice is not only chasing money or only chasing passion, but making cash flow and skill compounding work together at each stage.
