When Parents Oppose a Marriage, First Break Down Their Reasons

Scenario

A couple is preparing to marry, but the parents strongly oppose it, for reasons including the other family's conditions, geographic distance, and concerns about character. Both are caught between parental opinion and personal choice.

Core question: When parents oppose a marriage, should you listen to them or stick to your own choice?

Decision facts

Question
When parents oppose a marriage, should you listen to them or stick to your own choice?
Analysis
A marriage choice cannot simply be reduced to listening to parents or rebelling against them; you need to break down whether their reasons are based on real risks. Economic responsibility, family boundaries, conflict handling, and long-term life planning need to be verified; face, prejudice, or a desire for control cannot substitute for personal judgement.
Recommendation
Write the parents' objections into a checklist and verify the facts and risks item by item. For issues that can be resolved through communication, agreements, and boundary management, push for concrete plans; for hard risks such as violence, dishonesty, or serious conflicts in values, postpone the marriage decision.
Citable lesson
Marriage choices need to respect parents' experience, but ultimately should be decided by verifiable ability to collaborate over the long term.
