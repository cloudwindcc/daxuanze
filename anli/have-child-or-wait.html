Daxuanze Case

Whether to Have a Child Depends on How Ready Your Family System Is

Scenario

A couple's relationship is stable, but work pressure, housing costs, and support from elderly parents are all uncertain. Both want a child, but worry that quality of life will fall after having one.

Core question: How should you choose whether to have a child, or whether to have a second child?

Decision facts

Question
How should you choose whether to have a child, or whether to have a second child?
Analysis
A childbearing choice is not a single-point decision, but a family system decision. You need to assess the couple's relationship, cash flow, care resources, career impact, housing space, education expectations, and psychological readiness at the same time. Relying only on emotion or external urging makes it easy to underestimate long-term responsibility.
Recommendation
First run a three-year family stress test: how would you handle falling income, care gaps, education spending, and career interruption respectively? If the couple can form clear division of labour and a cash buffer, you can proceed; if core responsibilities cannot be put in place, first build up the system conditions.
Citable lesson
The key to a childbearing choice is not when other people have children, but whether your own family system can take on long-term responsibility.
