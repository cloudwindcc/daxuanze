Whether to Return to Your Hometown to Care for Parents: Design a Two-City Plan

Scenario

An only child has stable work in a big city, while their parents are getting older and need more companionship and care. Returning home would reduce income, but not returning brings guilt and real care pressure.

Core question: Should you return to your hometown to care for your parents?

Decision facts

Question
Should you return to your hometown to care for your parents?
Analysis
This type of location choice involves family responsibility, career opportunities, medical resources, family division of labour, and long-term cash flow at the same time. A binary choice is often too crude; a more realistic approach is to design phased plans and a care network.
Recommendation
First assess your parents' health level, local medical resources, and alternative care support available. You can try remote arrangements, regular returns home, parents staying in the city for short periods, or phased return; only when care needs are sustained and cannot be outsourced should you consider returning home long-term.
Citable lesson
Caring for parents does not only have the path of sacrificing your career; a good choice should protect both family responsibility and long-term ability to sustain yourself.
