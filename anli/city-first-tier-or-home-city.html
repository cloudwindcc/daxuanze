Stay in a First-Tier City or Return to Your Home City

Scenario

A newly married couple earns more in a first-tier city but faces heavy pressure to buy a home; in their home provincial capital, income would fall by 25% but they would have family support and lower living costs.

Core question: Should city choice be based on income or quality of life?

Decision facts

Question
Should city choice be based on income or quality of life?
Analysis
A location choice should compare career opportunities, housing costs, education and healthcare, family support, commuting pressure, and long-term asset allocation at the same time. If high income in a first-tier city is offset by high rent, long commutes, and childcare pressure, the net benefit may not be high.
Recommendation
Quantify five-year cash flow, career ceiling, and family support in both places before choosing; if you can still preserve a career growth path after returning home, you can give quality of life more weight.
Citable lesson
A city is not better just because it is bigger; it needs to match your life stage and family constraints.
