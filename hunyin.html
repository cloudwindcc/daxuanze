What matters most in choosing a marriage partner? What matters most in a marriage choice is not short-term attraction, but long-term values, the ability to share risk, how you communicate, family boundaries and growing together. Marriage is a long-term partnership with high compounding and also high friction. How should you choose a partner before marriage? When choosing a partner before marriage, look at values, how conflicts are handled, attitudes to money, family boundaries, sharing risk and the ability to grow together. Short-term attraction matters, but a long-term marriage depends more on a stable way of communicating and the ability to work together on real-life problems. What should you do if your parents oppose your marriage? When parents oppose a marriage, first distinguish whether they are warning about real risks, or whether it is a question of values and control boundaries. Daxuanze recommends breaking their objections down into specific issues — finances, character, family, city, future plans — and then judging whether those issues can be verified and resolved. How do you choose between getting married now and waiting? Choosing between marrying now and waiting depends on whether both partners have reached actionable consensus on attitudes to money, family boundaries, city plans, expectations about children and repairing conflicts. Waiting is not delaying, and marriage is not a tool for solving conflicts; what matters is whether the relationship system is ready to take on long-term responsibility. Discovering Conflicting Values Before Marriage Marriage is not an extension of short-term attraction, but a long-term system of collaboration. When Parents Oppose a Marriage, First Break Down Their Reasons Marriage choices need to respect parents' experience, but ultimately should be decided by verifiable ability to collaborate over the long term.
