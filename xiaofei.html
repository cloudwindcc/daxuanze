The Four Quadrants of Spending Decisions

High value, low price

Characteristics:Great value for money, high long-term value

Action strategy:Buy now, buy in bulk

Examples:Quality books, skills training, health investments

Mental accounting：Investing in yourself; worth the money

High value, high price

Characteristics:Excellent quality, worth investing in

Action strategy:Research thoroughly and find the best timing

Examples:Property, education, major equipment

Mental accounting：A major investment; decide carefully

Low value, low price

Characteristics:Optional, impulse purchases

Action strategy:Set a cooling-off period and delay gratification

Examples:Small accessories, snacks, impulse shopping

Mental accounting：Optional; avoid waste

Low value, high price

Characteristics:Vanity spending, brand premiums

Action strategy:Avoid firmly and look for alternatives

Examples:Luxury goods, face-saving projects, conspicuous consumption

Mental accounting：A vanity trap; stay away from the temptation

Real Spending Decision Cases

Case 1: A 30-year-old office worker's car purchase decision

Background:Age 30, annual income of 200,000 yuan, considering a new car at 300,000 yuan vs. a used car

Tools used:Spending value assessment + opportunity cost calculation

Analysis:A new car loses 150,000 yuan to depreciation; a used car saves 200,000 yuan that can be invested

Final decision:Chose a used car at 150,000 yuan and invested the remaining funds in an index fund, earning 80,000 yuan over 3 years

How do you judge whether something is worth buying? To judge whether something is worth buying, look at how often you will use it, the alternatives, maintenance costs, cash-flow pressure and long-term value. A high price does not mean it is not worth it, and a low price does not mean it is good value — what matters is whether it genuinely improves your life or your productivity. Buy a Car or Preserve Cash Flow The core of consumption decisions is matching value, not cheapness or expensiveness itself.
