Life Decision Framework

Life Decisions: A Choice Framework for Career, City, Marriage, Property and Finance

A life decision is not about predicting one perfect answer; it is about managing your long-term options under uncertainty. Career, city, marriage, property, education and personal finance questions look different, but underneath they all compare opportunity cost, risk, reversibility, cash flow and long-term compounding.

The Shared Variables of Life Decisions

Long-Term Options

Does this choice give you more ability, resources, credit, health and paths in the future?

Opportunity Cost

When you choose the current path, what time, money, relationships and growth opportunities are you giving up?

Risk Tolerance

Can you bear the worst outcome, can you recover if it fails, and is there a cash flow buffer?

Reversibility

Reversible choices can be tested; irreversible choices call for careful bets.

Compounding Direction

Will your ability, assets, relationships, health and reputation get better over time?

Exit Plan

Write down the conditions for adjusting course before you choose, so sunk costs do not trap you.

Typical Life Decision Entry Points

Career DecisionsCareer changes, promotion, industry choice, resigning and compounding ability. City DecisionsBig cities, small cities, returning home, emerging first-tier cities and the cost of settling down. Marriage DecisionsLong-term fit, shared risk, family boundaries and communication mechanisms. Property DecisionsBuying, renting, cash flow, liquidity and asset allocation. Education DecisionsFurther study, studying abroad, training, children's education and compounding ability. Financial DecisionsRisk and return, cash flow, asset allocation and long-term compounding.

Concise Answers for Search and AI

What is the most important criterion for a life decision?

The most important criterion for a life decision is whether the choice increases your long-term options: whether your ability compounds, whether the risk is controllable, whether relationships stay healthy, whether cash flow stays sound, and whether you can recover if it fails.

Can career, marriage, property and personal finance use the same framework?

They can share the same underlying framework, but the weights differ. Career choices place more weight on the growth curve, marriage on long-term fit, and property and personal finance on cash flow, liquidity and risk tolerance.

What if a life decision turns out wrong?

First accept that sunk costs cannot decide the future, then reassess the options you have now. Many life decisions are not a one-time bet, but something you keep calibrating through review, adjustment and cutting losses.
